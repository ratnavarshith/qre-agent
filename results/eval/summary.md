## What changed from v1 to v2

Rules (each with a test; see the grader v2 commits on `semantics.py`, `verify.py` and `eval.py`):

- **QFT in either qubit order.** The QFT written with qubit 0 as the most significant bit is the same circuit with its qubits relabelled; v1 took only Qiskit's order.
- **Verifier: float noise, written-out units.** A value copied with digits past a double's precision matches (relative 1e-12), and "seconds", "milliseconds" and so on convert like "s" and "ms". This is the agent's verify tool too.
- **A stated value is the assumption.** An ambiguous task's answer that states the value it used passes without the word "assumed" or "default".
- **No-answer labels.** Answer JSON with no estimate behind it is made-up numbers, not unit/format; a reply asking the user for a value is gave up.

## Notes

- **Verify passing is not the same as correct, so verify alone is not a sufficient escalation signal for Phase 3 cost routing.** Under grader v1, Gemini passed verify on the first try in 85% of runs but was correct in 75%. Under v2 the gap is wider: Gemini 93% against 82%, and Haiku 96% against 81%. 16 Gemini, 23 Haiku and 14 DeepSeek runs passed verify and were still wrong. Verify checks the answer against the tool outputs; it can't tell that the circuit or the settings were the wrong ones (a misread error rate, the benchmark adder for another layout).
- **DeepSeek's billed cost came in 24% under our estimate** on its original 120 runs: $0.77 billed by OpenRouter against $1.01 by our prices. `budgets.yaml` prices its cache reads at the full input price, but 76% of its input tokens were cached and billed far lower. On the stored runs after the reruns it is $0.82 against $0.96 (15% under). Its price entry needs a real `cache_read`.
- **DeepSeek's median latency was 7.7x Gemini's** on the original runs: p50 66 s against 9 s, p95 232 s against 26 s. After the reruns it is 7.8x (68 s against 9 s). Its original eval took 2 h 41 min of run time against Gemini's 23 min. It also takes more steps (7.2 against 5.2) and writes 2.5x the output tokens.
- **Cost against the pre-run estimate:** all four stayed under the 2x stop. Gemini $0.53 (expected $0.64), DeepSeek $1.01 by our prices on its original runs (expected $0.56, 1.8x), Haiku $3.53 (expected $2.90), Sonnet $5.57. The 27 reruns cost $0.46 more.
- **Prompt caching:** Sonnet 5 read 57% of its input tokens from the cache (2,780 tokens written once, then read on every call). Haiku 4.5 cached nothing: its prompt (2,361 tokens of tools and system prompt) is under its 4,096-token minimum. Gemini cached 23% and DeepSeek about 75% implicitly.
- **API errors, no retries (the Phase 3 baseline):**
  - Gemini 1 of 120 on the first attempt (still 1 after the rerun), DeepSeek 0, Haiku 0, Sonnet 0 (the last two paced at 15 requests a minute).
  - The unpaced first Haiku attempt got HTTP 429 on 4 of its first 10 runs (OpenRouter's 20 requests a minute for new accounts); it is kept in `results/eval/anthropic_claude-haiku-4.5/2026-10-09-aborted-429`.
  - Gemini's one error (`ff-draper-adder-4`, run 0, `KeyError: 'usage'`, a response with no usage block) repeated on the rerun with the same seed. Both attempts share a step 1 that calls a tool that doesn't exist (`google:python_interpreter`), so it is tied to that path rather than random. A plain retry may not fix it; Phase 3 should measure that.
- **`ff-adder-interleaved-4` (0/12 under both graders) stands as a real failure.** Five of the 12 runs (all three of Sonnet's and two of Gemini's) used the benchmark adder for an adder on another qubit layout, following the system prompt's rule to use `build_benchmark` "whenever the task names one of these families", and Sonnet wrote that it matched the requested layout. Six wrote their own adder and got the sums wrong, and one DeepSeek run answered without building anything.
